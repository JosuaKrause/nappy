# crisp-beaver — On a pedestrian street a hard seal closes the street completely, and no soft seal is placed there · filed 2026-11-01

[tiny-lark](../../playtests/2026-11-01-tiny-lark.md), #931: "(c)", answering the question of what a soft seal should leave open on a pedestrian street (no cars): (a) still leave the middle open; (b) close the street completely; (c) never place a soft seal on a pedestrian street.

**Context.** Split from tiny-alpaca (what a seal leaves open on a pedestrian street), closed in the queue review (inbox #922 in tiny-lark). Found by the review of #727 (M219, on a pedestrian street a blocked walker turns round), not reported by the player: "Soft seals (`SS..SS`) and two-body hard seals (`OO..OO`, such as a pair of `cafe_tables`, `market_stall` or `delivery_van`) are placed in a precinct as if lanes 2-3 were a carriageway. So a seal in a pedestrian zone has a two-lane hole that walkers walk through (seed 67: days 2-8 and 10-13). Whether the player can pass through it too is not checked here."

The [M64 seal pictures record](../../decisions/2026-09-09-M64-eight-seal-pictures.md) gives a seal two strengths: "A **hard** seal spans the street frontage to frontage and nothing gets past it", and "A **soft** seal is the obstacle pair: both pavements taken, and the carriageway still there to be risked". The [M110 record](../../decisions/2026-09-12-M110-the-crowd-goes-round-a-seal-every-solid-body.md) keeps the crowd off a soft seal's footways while "the other footway and the carriageway stay open, which is the whole difference between a body and a seal".

**Read as:** on a pedestrian street a hard seal closes the street completely (a two-lane gap in it contradicts the M64 record and is a defect, which nobody was asked about), and no soft seal is placed there at all. *Proposed, not asked for:* the band `next`, the closed entry's own.
