# tiny-alpaca — What a seal leaves open on a pedestrian street · filed 2026-10-11

Found by the review of [M219](../../decisions/2026-09-26-M219.md)'s pull request (#727), not
reported by the player: "Soft seals (`SS..SS`) and two-body hard seals (`OO..OO`, such as a pair of
`cafe_tables`, `market_stall` or `delivery_van`) are placed in a precinct as if lanes 2-3 were a
carriageway. So a seal in a pedestrian zone has a two-lane hole that walkers walk through (seed 67:
days 2-8 and 10-13). Whether the player can pass through it too is not checked here."

**What is decided.** The [M64 seal pictures record](../../decisions/2026-09-09-M64-eight-seal-pictures.md)
gives a seal two strengths: "A **hard** seal spans the street frontage to frontage and nothing gets
past it", and "A **soft** seal is the obstacle pair: both pavements taken, and the carriageway still
there to be risked"; the [M110 record](../../decisions/2026-09-12-M110-the-crowd-goes-round-a-seal-every-solid-body.md)
keeps the crowd off a soft seal's footways while "the other footway and the carriageway stay open,
which is the whole difference between a body and a seal".

**The two cases differ.**

- **A two-body hard seal with a gap** contradicts "nothing gets past it" if she or a walker can pass
  between its bodies. That is a defect against the M64 record as it stands.
- **A soft seal's open middle lanes** may be the M64 rule applied literally: a precinct's lanes 2
  and 3 stand where a street's carriageway would. But a precinct has no traffic, so the gap is risked
  for nothing, and whether a soft seal on a street with no carriageway should leave anything open is
  a question the records do not answer. It goes to the player before anything is built.

*Proposed, not asked for:* first find out, headless on one of seed 67's days, whether she can pass
each kind of gap; then fix the hard seal so it spans the precinct, with a test that places one and
finds no lane open; and put the soft seal's question to the player with what passing it costs her.

**The same review saw walkers inside bodies**: "a few walker-frames inside bodies on seed 67 days 2
and 7 at x=24 are identical on main". Nothing says yet which bodies or why. *Proposed, not asked for:*
it is filed here because it was found the same way, not because it has the same cause; the work is
finding which bodies hold a walker inside them and either fixing it or splitting it into an entry of
its own once the cause is known.

*Proposed, not asked for:* the band `next`, and the findings as one entry.
