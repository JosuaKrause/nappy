# minty-quail — Events are composed of behaviours, each streaming its own state (events-composed 2a/4) · 2026-10-25


minty-quail's `events-composed.md`, the first half of its second PR, split as the approved plan
allows (PR #848). The item: `EventInstance._process` ran one movement branch chosen by flag
combinations, the pursuit, notice, investigation and flock rules were instance methods, a stream-out
carried a handful of hand-picked fields, and the masked man's warning was a counter of its own beside
`Warning`, whose called-off and run-out half had no caller. The player left the shape of the classes
to the builder and asked that composition be considered (minty-lemur #797).

**Built.** What an event does is a list of behaviours under `src/events/behaviours/`, composed per
catalogue row from the row's data:
- `EventBehaviour` (RefCounted) owns one rule and the state it changes, with `step()`,
  `step_leaving()`, `save()` and `restore()`. There is no class per event type.
- `EventBehaviours.compose(event, row)` builds the list in tick order and sets the instance's typed
  field for each one (`pursuit`, `flock`, `hold`, ...), so the rest of the game asks the event.
- The ten: `EventDeparture` (every row: the way out of sight, a walked way, the sight rule),
  `EventRouteWalk` (mobile rows; it keeps no state of its own), `EventFlock` (the pigeons' birds),
  `EventInvestigation` (the heated patrol's stop, owed men and junction stop), `EventPursuit` (the
  chase: notice, stand-off, held lunge, giving up when she runs), `EventOfficer` (a man's stop, his
  trail and walk back), `EventNoticeRun` (the unclocked wait of the mouse, the pigeons and the masked
  man, his warning on his line, the flock's flush), `EventHold` (the chat or door hold's clock),
  `EventUnreachable` (retiring the loose dog that cannot reach her), `EventAbduction` (the van's take).
- `_process` steps the composed list. Leaving, it runs `EventDeparture.leave()` and each
  behaviour's `step_leaving()`; in a hold, only `EventHold.step()`. The composition matches the old
  dispatch row for row, so no row gains or loses a behaviour.
- Streaming: `EventInstance.save_behaviours()` keys each behaviour's `save()` by its class name;
  `EventManager._stream_out()` puts it on `EventScheduler.Planned.behaviours`, `_stream_in()` puts it
  back after `resume()`, and a repeating plan starting over clears it. Another instance a behaviour
  holds (an officer's vehicle, a vehicle's live men) is not carried.
- The masked man's warning is a `Warning` with `cancel_if` (she is back outside his notice) and
  `on_expired` (he runs the moment she is inside his run distance, the warning staying up with no
  length), driven by `tick()`. His own counter is gone; what he does is unchanged.

**Choices made where the design was silent, open to overturn:**
- A behaviour is part of its event, not a client of it: it reads and moves the event's body through
  the instance's own private members and changes the phase only through `transition()`, since PR 3
  moves simulation onto a public `step()` and narrowing the surface first would rename it twice.
- The plan's `on_phase()` hook is not built: what a phase change owes stays in `transition()`, and no
  behaviour reacts to a change it did not cause.
- `EventDeparture` and `EventHold` are in the list but do not step in the ordinary tick
  (`steps_each_tick = false`); `_process` runs them in branches of their own.
- Saved state is keyed by the behaviour's class name, and a behaviour the state does not name keeps
  its fresh state.
