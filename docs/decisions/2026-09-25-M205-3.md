# M205 — The man shouting lights his halo on the web · built 2026-10-04

*([PLAYTEST-140](../playtests/PLAYTEST-140.md), statement 4: "the yeller has no effect on the meter
and its halo doesn't even turn on", v0.18.0 on a phone; [PLAYTEST-144](../playtests/PLAYTEST-144.md),
statements 21, 24 and 27, the last with no halo in desktop Chrome on the released page. Asked on
2026-10-04 whether they had checked it again, the player: "hmm, I gave you all the info on that
already. what else do you need?" (inbox #514 in [azure-tapir](../playtests/2026-10-04-azure-tapir.md)), so it was reproduced from the recorded evidence.)*

**Built (PR #525).** In desktop Chrome, on the released page (v0.23.0) and on local release exports,
the man shouting charges the meter (the readout's `incoming` line reads 18.6 to 29.4/s with him at 94
to 147px, and nothing past his 210px reach), but **his rim never draws**, while a walker's and a
car's rims beside him do.

**The cause is a ceiling the Compatibility renderer's canvas shader has, which the browser
enforces.** Every canvas item that draws with an `instance uniform` gets a 16-`vec4` block, first
free block first, and the canvas shader declares only 256 `vec4`s for them (Godot 4.7.2,
`MAX_GLOBAL_SHADER_UNIFORMS` in `drivers/gles3/rasterizer_canvas_gles3.cpp`, with no platform
condition), so only sixteen rims are readable at once. The project uses the same renderer on the
desktop, but a desktop GL driver happens to read past the array, while WebGL reads zero: a rim given
a later block draws nothing there, with no error. `EventInstance` built a rim for every live event as
it entered the tree, about 45 at dawn, so most events, him included, held blocks the browser could
not read; the crowd built its rims only when needed and often got freed low blocks.

**The fix.** A rim exists only while it is wanted, and never more than `EntityHalo.RIM_BUDGET` (15,
against `COMPATIBILITY_RIM_BLOCKS`, 16) are alive at once:

- `EventInstance` builds its rim on its first nonzero glow, as `CrowdAgent` already did. An owner
  frees its own rim once it has faded out, on the spot (`EntityHalo.release()`: `remove_child()`
  then `free()`, never `queue_free()`), so its block comes back before any rim built later that
  frame takes one. `ExcitementHalo` frees at once the rim of a holder already queued for deletion,
  and never lights such a pick.
- `ExcitementHalo` counts last frame's holders, including one that has left the candidate set,
  which is told zero and stays counted until its fade ends.
- When the budget is full, a new pick waits rather than taking a block. A pick lit last frame and
  still picked stays lit; every other pick joins a line, oldest first and strongest first among
  those that began waiting on the same frame, and is told zero meanwhile, so a pick whose rim is
  still fading keeps fading in line and is relit in place if its turn comes first. A wait happens
  only when eight or more rims are still fading while eight are picked, and ends within one fade
  (0.8s) plus a frame or two, however fast the picks change.
- **No fade is cut short**: the **cues** skill's "Both channels ease rather than jump" holds without
  an exception.

M89's single shared material and its `instance uniform` stay. `tests/test_halo.gd` holds every rim
alive at any moment within the budget on a walk past forty live events, an event's rim held only
while picked, a waiting pick lit within one fade under fast turnover, and a dropped holder fading
and giving its block back. The first three fail on the code before the fix; the last fails with the
loop over holders no longer picked removed. The **godot** skill carries the
sixteen-rim ceiling as a trap. Before-and-after stills, the probes and the scripts that rerun the
browser comparison are in `docs/evidence/m205-yeller-halo-web-2026-10-04/`; the browser run lights
him and never fills the budget (at most four rims alive), so a pick waiting for a block is shown by
the tests and the engine source, not in a browser.

**The meter half did not reproduce.** In every browser run he charges her while she is in his
reach, so his row, the meter and `docs/COSTS.md` are unchanged. The review item asks the player,
after the release, whether the meter moves beside him on the phone.

**Proposed, not asked for, and open to overturn:** the budget of 15, keeping one block for the
throwaway quad `main.gd`'s shader warm pass draws with the same material; a new pick waiting for a
block when the budget is full; the line served oldest first rather than strongest first, so a
strong pick that arrives while a weaker one waits is lit after it; and a re-picked rim that is still fading going on fading while
others wait, so that the wait stays within one fade; keeping the `instance uniform` rather than
moving the rim's colour to a vertex-colour channel, which would lift the ceiling but tint each rim by
its draw's own colour.
