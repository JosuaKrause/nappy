# minty-quail — Every walk starts through one start_day, planned by DayPlanner; the baby's excitement answered by the manager (events-composed 4b/4) · 2026-10-31


minty-quail's `events-composed.md`, the second of three PRs that build its fourth (PR #880), after
4a's split of the manager into its parts (minty-quail-24). The item: the escape city was planned
beside the day, by `FinalePlanner` (routes) running the seal planner and the scheduler, and hosted by
a second entry, `EventManager.start_planned()`, which the building's walk used too with
`BuildingPlan.plan()`'s placements; `City` and `InteriorScene` each summed their events for the baby;
`BuildingPlan.lay_the_grates()`, a placement class, built the grates' sprites; `WalkSiting` always read
the day's home-street rule. The player asked for dependency injection and a layered design
(minty-lemur #785, #794) and that no old name be kept so callers or tests need not change (inbox #852
in minty-dolphin). The orchestrating session's picks for this PR, open to overturn: the manager
answers the baby's excitement (pick A, the fence widened to the baby, the world context and the fake
worlds), and a `DayPlanner` holds every planning half.

**Built.**
- **One entry for every walk.** `EventManager.start_day(spec, rng, consumed, focus)` starts a day,
  the escape's city and the escape's building; `start_planned()` is gone with no forwarder. The
  walk's day is decided once, from the plan: a day's plan streams under the spec's own number, the
  escape's sections under the run's last day, counting encounters under their own number, and
  `DayPlanner.plan()` asserts that a spec closes streets exactly when it is a day's. The old check
  that a spec's day matched the day started went with the separate day argument: rigs and tests
  start any day on a run at day 1, so the spec's own number is the day started (a check comparing it
  with the run's day failed every shard). Every caller moved: main's escape city and building,
  `DayDirector`, `tests/building_events.gd`, and the test and probe calls, which pass
  `DaySpec.for_day(day)`. `start_recipe()` stays the authored scenes' own entry and shares the one
  start with `start_day()`: `_begin(spec)` clears and sets the walk up before planning,
  `_take(spec, plan, focus)` takes the placements, records the bodies, starts the director and
  streams; the review found a hand copy of that half in `start_recipe()` and it went.
  `EventManager.walk()` returns the escape's chains, which main reads for the kerb tint and the
  run-log summary.
- **`DayPlanner`** (`src/events/day_planner.gd`), handed the shared `EventWorld` like the manager's
  other parts, plans a day, the escape's city or its building on the same streams in the same order
  as before, picked by the spec: the building by its world, the escape's city by `closes_streets`
  false, a day otherwise (no new `DaySpec` field, since `src/day/` was outside the fence).
  `FinalePlanner` is the route alone: `plan()` and `Plan.placements` are gone, and `Plan.summary()`
  takes the placement count, so the run-log line is unchanged. `BuildingPlan.plan()` takes a random
  stream rather than a seed. Tests that hand placements in on purpose (test_finale's crater and
  owed-return tests, test_interior's single bang) hand the manager a planner of their own
  (`tests/given_planner.gd`) through `EventManager.planner`, then start the walk the ordinary way.
- **The baby's excitement.** `City` and `InteriorScene` sum nothing; `WorldContext` holds the
  `events` and `crowd` fields and hands the baby the manager's answer plus the crowd's, stating the
  door rule once. The two test worlds that only forwarded to a manager are gone; tests hand a bare
  `WorldContext` the manager.
- **The grates.** `BuildingPlan.grates()` says where they lie; `SteamGrates.lay()` in
  `src/events/views/` makes the sprites, with the same picture, centring, name and draw layer, in the
  views because the picture is on the events atlas page.
- **`WalkSiting`** reads `holds_the_home_streets` from the spec, handed through `EventsAhead`. Only day
  specs reach it, so nothing in play changes. New suite `tests/test_walk_siting_spec.gd`: the siting
  `EventsAhead` hands out flips with the spec both ways, and a source-text check requires each of its
  four `WalkSiting` builds to copy the rule (three are not reachable from outside).
- The orchestrator's queue move fixed two sentences the move made untrue: the Steam row of
  `docs/GRAPHICS.md` and the caller named in `src/routes/seal_planner.gd`'s comment.
- Sentences naming the excitement functions on `City` name them on `WorldContext`, which `City`
  extends: the city skill, `docs/MECHANICS.md` and `docs/CITY.md`.

**Verified at test parity** (inbox #813 in curly-moose), headless on macOS with Godot 4.7.2. The
placement fingerprint (`tests/probes/placement_fingerprint.gd`, committed first against the
unchanged code) prints every placement of walks started through a real `City` or `InteriorScene` and
its manager: the escape city on minty-quail-8's eight seeds on the day-1 and the day-14 stream, the
building on three seeds, and seven days on two seeds. Its output was identical before, after the
first item and with every item in. `check.sh`, `lint.sh`, `ci_telemetry_kinds.py` and
`cost-table.sh --check` pass; the touched suites, two or three per Godot call, report no failure and
no `SCRIPT ERROR`; CI runs the full suite on the merge result. No behaviour changes on purpose, so the
remeasuring step gains nothing.

**Kept as before, open to a later look:** the escape's walk records no bodies in the obstruction
record, hands its planning no run-log trace, and spends none of the run's one-shots.

**The fork left open:** `WorldContext` (the world layer) holds fields typed `EventManager` and `Crowd`
(simulation), a typed dependency pointing up a layer. It goes to shared-components, whose
`ExcitementSource` component is what those fields are typed against once it exists; the plainer
alternative is the world handed callables (`shared-components.md`).
