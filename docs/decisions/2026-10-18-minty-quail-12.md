# minty-quail — Walker and car are one mover with two strategies · 2026-10-25


minty-quail's `shared-components.md`, the first of two PRs for its part "Walker and car are one
class" (PR #847). `CrowdAgent` (`src/crowd/crowd_agent.gd`) switched on its `Kind` 66 times, and
`_cannot_go_on`/`_may_stand_on` mixed the car's seal logic with the walker's footway logic.

**Built** (`src/crowd/`). `LaneStrategy` is the set of questions the mover asks its kind, with the
neutral answers; `WalkerStrategy` (footways, sidesteps, corners, the checkpoint huts a walker
queues at) and `CarStrategy` (give-way, planned turns, the spine's tunnel and bridge, queue
claims) answer them and hold the state only their kind has. `CrowdAgent` stays the class every
caller names; `kind` is a property that installs the strategy, so a hand-built agent that sets
its kind after `new()` has that kind's rules. The mover keeps what both kinds share and every
frame reads: the lane state, the arc a car follows, the steering offset `_detour`, the look-ahead,
the held step, placement and recycling, the junction and queue questions, `heading()` and
`velocity()`. Each kind's `cannot_go_on()` is stated whole in its own strategy, and
`may_stand_on()` is one line each. 50 of the 66 branches are gone; the 11 that choose a drawing
are the second PR's, and the 5 that choose the excitement field's numbers (the car's or the
pedestrian's radii and intensity, the top speed, and "never lethal for a walker") are left for
`ExcitementSource`. The 24 excitement methods shared by name with `EventInstance` are untouched.
Outside `src/crowd` only comments changed, each naming a member where it now lives.

**Verified at test parity** (inbox #813 in curly-moose). `tests/probes/minty_quail_lane_parity.gd`
runs the m159 crowd probe's walk on seeds 478156010 and 4242, days 1, 9 and 14, the street walk and
the walk at the nearest walker, 150 warm-up and 600 measured ticks, and chains SHA-256 over every
agent's position and speed after every tick, plus a second chain over the redraw key, the
drawn-heading key, visibility, lane key, velocity, heading and colour. Against the base 167af592,
the branch before its merge with main (b6e0a6c5) matched both chains in all 12 cases on every one
of 6 runs per case; after the merge (37517b84 against main at ee7f15d1, 3 runs per case) both
chains matched in all 12 cases. `tests/probes/m159_tick_sweep_events_cost.gd` (every event, agent,
her alert and the baby's meters per tick; the same seeds and days, both walks, 3 repetitions, both
modes): all 72 chains at b6e0a6c5 matched 167af592. `tests/probes/m152_car_jumps.gd`'s 90 report
lines were identical at the two. The whole suite shard by shard at b6e0a6c5 had 0 failures but one
`test_texts_catalogue` check, whose allow-list did not name `car_strategy.gd` for the moved
placement message; with that fixed (bbdcc61b) shard 8 had 0 failures. Tests changed only as far as
the code moved: per-kind state is read through `strategy`, and a car no longer carries a yield or a
door state, so `test_prediction_reuse` and `test_crowd_contributions` mutate those on walkers only.

The first build was slower in the agents' own frame: in the headless debug build, reading another
object's field costs about seven times a member read. Without changing a value, the walker's yield
and door factors are asked only when they can be other than one, the hold-still question is part of
`begin_frame()`, the nose, the barrier distance and the scan origin are cached on the mover when a
strategy is installed, a car's redraw reads `velocity()` once, and Crowd hands a walker its door
frame through the strategy. Measured with the same probe, per-tick medians over 3 runs per case,
headless on an Apple M2, base and head interleaved per round: in the first round the agents' own
frame was 1–4% lower at b6e0a6c5 in every case, and the crowd tick 2–4% lower on day 1, within 1% on
day 9 and 0.5–2% higher on day 14. The second round is not evidence: the machine sped up between
and during the rounds, and every head run in it came out faster. The run after the merge is not
evidence either way: another worktree's suite ran on the machine at the same time, and the crowd
tick, which this change barely touches, moved as much as the agents' frame.

**Choices made where the design was silent, open to overturn:** the class callers name stays
`CrowdAgent`, with the `LaneMover` base to come in the second PR (the orchestrating session's
pick); the per-frame inputs `Crowd` writes for cars (`junction_hold`, `gate_hold`, `gap_ahead`,
`leader_speed`, `pedestrian_ahead`) and for walkers at a door (`door_ahead`, `door_ahead_along`)
stay fields of `CrowdAgent` (the orchestrating session's pick); the arc a car follows and the
steering offset stay on the mover, since `heading()` and the cross-steer read them every frame; a
strategy holds its mover and reads its lane fields directly, rather than the lane state being
renamed public; `kind` stays settable after construction; three fixed facts of a kind (the nose,
the barrier distance and where the look-ahead starts) are cached on the mover for speed, so where
the look-ahead starts is a cached flag rather than a strategy call; `Crowd`'s own kind checks stay,
as the crowd's decisions about each kind; the unused `_room_beyond_the_map()` became the car's
`has_left_the_map()`; the crowd's lane movement stays off the world's `Navigator`, since a
lane-following held step is a different motion model from the navigator's swept step and moving
it cannot stay at parity (the orchestrating session's pick; it stays queued).

**For the remeasuring step:** no behaviour change is meant. The crowd tick on day 14 measured
0.5–2% higher in the interleaved round.
