# minty-quail — The crowd steps on the physics tick, drawn interpolated (shared-components C1) · 2026-10-31


minty-quail's `shared-components.md`, its seventh PR (PR #890) under the approved plan, after D1
(minty-quail-32), and the M100 item "`CrowdAgent` moves at frame rate; every rule about that movement
is applied at physics rate" (M100, small, real and nobody's), which it closes. The player asked that
every clock making a time the player sees or deciding an outcome count the fixed 30-a-second physics
ticks, so that on a lagging machine the game slows down rather than running on (minty-dolphin #866).
The M100 item: a crowd agent moved in `_process`, once a drawn frame, while every rule governing that
movement ran in `Crowd._physics_process`, so how far a car travelled between two applications of "nothing
enters a box it cannot leave" varied with the machine (about 3.7 steps per right-of-way pass at 110 fps,
1.0 at 30). The audit's recommendation (`docs/evidence/audit-2026-09-13/AUDIT.md`, finding 3.1) was to
move the agents onto the tick, at the cost of drawing the crowd at thirty frames a second; the plan's F4
(a), the orchestrating session's pick and open to overturn, kept the smooth drawing by physics
interpolation.

**Built.**
- `LaneMover._process()` is the public `step(delta)`, and `Crowd._tick_the_crowd()` runs the game's
  order as it stood: it spaces the traffic, holds walkers at doors and gives way at junctions
  (`_advance_the_world()`), meets the player (strike, horn, bump, make-way), then steps every agent
  (`_step_the_agents()`); `Crowd.step()`, the rig's tick, runs the same order less the player half. The
  first build stepped the agents first, the order the rig had used, and that alone raised box collisions
  for a 30 fps player from about 2.0 to 3.1 a minute (48 paired runs); the review's measurements sent it
  back to the game's order, which reproduces the crowd before this change exactly at 30 fps, as the probes
  run it; in the whole game, the systems that tick after the crowd (the event manager, the baby, a scene
  recipe's runtime) read it one step later than before, so a scene can play out differently, the same on
  every machine (a scene recipe's car traced at 30, 60 and 120 fps follows one path). Every clock
  inside the crowd advances by the tick.
  `--skip motion` is one gate on the crowd's tick; the agent's own flag is gone.
- The agents are drawn interpolated: the `PHYSICS_INTERPOLATION_MODE_OFF` override is gone, and
  `reset_physics_interpolation()` is called when an agent is made, at the end of every recycle, and when a
  walker comes out of a checkpoint hut, after it is shown again, since Godot drops a reset on a node hidden
  in the tree. An about-face, a turn's arc and a car crossing to its other lane are continuous and need
  none. A recording still turns interpolation off (`main.gd`). The morning's overlap resolve, which
  nudges cars apart after they enter the tree, is followed by a reset of every agent, on a day's start and
  a checkpoint retry alike (the review found 3 to 6 of 234 cars drawn sliding up to 76px on day 1's first
  frame).
- The crowd's tick is timed once in the frame record; an agent keeps its `CrowdTiming` only to count its
  draws. The redraw gate stays on the tick, since nothing it reads changes between ticks, so a car's bob
  and a walker's gait are redrawn at 30 Hz (open to overturn: redrawing every agent on every drawn frame
  would bring back the per-frame cost this change removed); a marked car's breathing caret, a danger cue
  on a few cars, breathes on every drawn frame, reading the jolt a fraction of a tick ahead, draw-only.
- Left as the builder chose where the design was silent, open to overturn: the crowd steps its agents
  inside its own tick, as the event manager does, rather than each agent having a `_physics_process`; the
  per-agent timing removed rather than nested; clocks kept in seconds advanced by the tick, as
  events-composed did; `scene-recipes/task-09-crossing.json`'s crowd observation moved from tick 308 to
  370, since the whole game at 30 fps is not tick-identical to the old one (the systems that tick after
  the crowd read it a step later), so the scene's pinned car arrives on another schedule and stands at the
  district door's gate at tick 308, where on the old code it was crossing the picture.
- Skill fixes: **crowd-traffic**'s rig paragraph named `agent._process(step)` and "the frame around the
  agents" and now names `agent.step(step)` and says the rig's tick and the live tick run one order, and
  `TrafficIndex` is a tick stale rather than a frame; the
  **telemetry** skill's paragraph and code block on a crowd agent timing itself are gone, replaced by a
  sentence saying it steps inside the crowd's timed tick. `frame_record.gd`'s and `frame_recorder.gd`'s
  crowd bucket and `README.md`'s `--skip motion` row name the crowd's tick.

**Measured** (`docs/evidence/minty-quail-crowd-tick-2026-10-31/`), headless and windowed on macOS with
Godot 4.7.2:
- At 30 fps the crowd is as before: `tests/probes/minty_quail_crowd_cadence.gd` over 12 cities, days 1
  and 9 and two walks (48 one-minute runs) is identical base against head on every run and every measure
  (horns, box collisions, bumps, strikes, box entries, mean speed, headways), and
  `minty_quail_lane_parity`, which now runs the tick and then the agents on both sides, the game's order,
  has identical position and drawn-state chains in all 12 runs.
- Smooth at 120 fps (`tests/probes/minty_quail_crowd_frames.gd`, 12 consecutive drawn frames of one
  street crop): the car's node moves only on tick frames, and all 9 frames drawn without a tick still
  differ from the frame before, by about 2,700 to 3,000 of the crop's 36,000 pixels. The two runs follow
  different cars.
- Cost (`m159_crowd_tick_cost`): the crowd's per-tick work grew (day 1, 0.30 to 2.80 ms) and its per-frame
  work went (2.52 ms before), so its total per second fell: day 1 from 160 to 84 ms at 60 fps and 312 to
  84 ms at 120 fps; day 9 from 40 to 23 and 73 to 23; day 14 from 68 to 39 and 125 to 39.
- At 120 fps against the game before (the same 48 runs, paired), a fast screen now plays as a 30 fps
  one does: horns 5.04 to 4.92 a minute, box collisions 2.85 to 2.00, bumps 7.10 to 6.98, strikes 0.52 to
  0.42, mean car speed 100.2 to 101.9px/s, box entries 299 to 311 a minute. Only the box entries are past
  twice their error, and one or two of seven would be by chance. The 120 fps rows before are emulated by
  stepping the agents four times a tick in a headless rig, not measured on a display. An 8-run table from
  the first build, which read as one collision rate of about 2.5 on every machine, did not survive the
  48 runs.
- The lethal carriageway's fairness contract, the green wave and the horn contract hold
  (`test_crowd`'s traffic contract, lights, signal and horn-watch tests, `test_blackout`,
  `test_police_car`, `test_fire_truck_body`). `check.sh`, `lint.sh`, `ci_telemetry_kinds.py` and
  `cost-table.sh --check` pass; the touched suites report no failure.
