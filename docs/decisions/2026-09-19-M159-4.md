# M159 — Nearby scenery residency and distant visual eviction · 2026-09-30

The player requests implementation of the measured nearby preparation and distant unloading
policy, then points to PR #444 as the existing investigation. Their full words are in
[snowy-pelican, implement nearby scenery loading and unloading](../playtests/2026-09-30-snowy-pelican.md).
The baseline remains the [investigation record](2026-09-19-M159-3.md) and its retained evidence;
this work does not repeat the preparation, memory or generated-route coverage survey.

Map-specific ground occupies freeable TileMapLayer chunks sharing the loading-time composed
TileSet. Shared baked pages and shader warmup remain resident at their permitted loading
moments. Clearing a growing TileMapLayer was rejected because #444 demonstrates that it
retains allocation; freeing chunks releases their map-specific cell storage. Cross-day ground
composition reuse is not required.

The initial home-centered viewport receives nearby scenery plus an off-screen margin.
Scenery loads within a smaller boundary and unloads beyond a wider one. Between them,
resident scenery stays resident and absent scenery stays absent. Building identities,
collision, frontage and entrance facts remain available for gameplay and poster placement;
window arrays, roof drawings and retained commands are prepared nearby and released far away.
Props, stacks, doors, signal artwork and closure markers retain their identities while their
drawing commands follow residency. Off-screen traffic signals and gameplay continue normally.

Ground, shadow and decal preparation shares the residency queue. Current city state drives
reconstruction after day changes, closure and block changes, emptied tree pits, posters,
blackout and home seals. Seeded appearance uses local deterministic streams; approach and
eviction do not consume gameplay RNG. Water and roof animation use a pausable city clock so
returning scenery matches the current phase. Eligibility accounts for full artwork bounds,
including roof extensions and station stacks.

Camera relocation and day/finale transitions prepare the destination before play resumes.
The ordinary approach queue has a work budget; preparation inside the safety guard and
explicit destination preparation are synchronous correctness safeguards rather than a hard
bound on complete-frame work.

The implementation chooses eight-by-eight-cell ground chunks (256px), a 256px loading
margin, a 512px retention margin, a 96px safety guard and a soft 2ms preparation budget.
These details were unchosen in the player's request and remain open to overturn. At the
168px/s running speed the loading margin gives about 1.52s of lead; the safety guard covers
the 92px maximum facing look-ahead reversal, leaving about 0.95s for ordinary preparation.
Residency maintenance advances after 16px of camera movement or while work remains queued.

The [implementation acceptance report](../evidence/m159-lazy-scenery-implementation-2026-09-30/README.md)
retains three clean native camera-model runs on two generated daily routes. They cover
17,024–17,408px out and back, with no missing ground or buildings and no ordinary safety-guard
catch-ups. Home holds 30 ground chunks, 1,432–1,460 static cells and 14 of 150–158 buildings;
peak ground residency is 42 chunks. Eviction invalidates the held ground nodes and releases
675,592–772,064 tracked Godot static bytes. This is not browser heap, GPU memory or process RSS.
The worst atomic ground batch is 0.329–0.332ms; camera-model update medians are 0.392–0.510ms,
with maxima 2.207–2.256ms under the soft budget. The model includes residency bookkeeping
and chunk preparation, bypasses production maintenance cadence, and excludes deferred drawing.

Two rendered gameplay bursts retain their original run folders and timing-based short clips.
The arterial pre-burst five-second window contains 187.7px travel, with callback intervals
of 5.115ms median and 8.990ms maximum. The shore's pre-burst window travels only 2.1px against
an event, so its timing is not walking evidence; its reversal burst supplies shoreline visual
evidence. These native rendering callback observations have no pre-change control and are not
a causal whole-frame improvement or a measure of physical presentation. The report retains
the complete distributions, source identity, reproduction and limitations.

Focused headless checks cover identity and collision, local RNG, current unloaded state,
hysteresis, real Main day resets, shared water clock, full/streamed shadow equivalence and
route tint after loading, eviction, return and finale. The CI route-tint failure exposes a
fixture that reads unprepared cells as though the whole city is loaded. It now visits every
neighborhood and compares the complete independently expected route-tint set, including
return and finale, preserving the assertion rather than shrinking it to nearby cells.

The orchestration skill's brief-pause instruction names `SendMessage`, but Codex's
`send_message` queues a message without starting an interrupted agent. In this session the
implementation agent was interrupted while the evidence-upload discussion continued;
queued scope updates did not resume it. The skill now distinguishes running agents from warm
idle or interrupted agents, which require `followup_task`. This changes neither ownership
nor the cold-agent replacement rule.
