# M226 — Every off-screen warning is a short badge, then the thing spawns off screen · 2026-10-05


The player's words: [PLAYTEST-145](../playtests/PLAYTEST-145.md) (the day-3 dog's timing is the gold
timing; statements 18–23 on what telegraphs), [calm-kestrel](../playtests/2026-10-04-calm-kestrel.md)
and [busy-quail](../playtests/2026-10-04-busy-quail.md) (inbox #559, #569: a badge alone for a short
time with nothing placed, then the thing placed off screen; "1s warning should be enough"), and
inbox #598 in [olive-hedgehog](../playtests/2026-10-05-olive-hedgehog.md), said while this was built:
"the telegraphing rule was about heavy penalty not *only* lethal", "fire truck has heavy penalty",
"the chase is what happens on screen", "0.53 is a good warning time", "I don't want any pop in",
"off screen is not the same as visible -- the corners get removed for visible not for off screen",
"why would the robber walk towards her when it spawns as pursuing robber? the proximity rule is only
for standing robbers", and "pursuers should never (or a long time) stop pursuing if she walks".

**What telegraphs.** A thing warns of its coming only if it goes fast (faster than her walk), comes
toward her, and carries a heavy penalty — it can end the day, or one pass at a walk costs a quarter
of the meter or more (the quarter is the build's line, open to overturn). The cyclist and the
pelican, the fire truck, the day-13 column and the planned convoy, the charging dog and every lethal
pursuer telegraph; the loose dog, the cat, the alley mouse, the pigeon flock and the police patrol do
not. `EventDef.telegraphs = false` removes the coming badge and warning-first placement; it does
not remove ordinary in-world telegraphs or placement validation. The flock, mouse, cat and patrol
keep those contracts. Only the loose dog's unannounced down-her-line arrival bypasses its spent
telegraph: it is created at once off screen down her sidewalk, its pass cost unchanged. Each row's
numbers and reasoning are in [EVENTS, What telegraphs](../EVENTS.md#what-telegraphs), which the
review item asks the player to check. The flock can cost a heavy hit but does not come toward her.

**Every warning from off screen is a short badge alone, then the thing spawns off screen.** The badge
shows with nothing in the world for at most a second (`Tuning.WARNING_ALONE_MAX`, checked in
`EventDef.validate()`); its place is fixed against her, so it never jumps; then the thing is created
wholly outside the camera's whole view — everything it can draw, every view, shadow and halo rim
and the full danger caret (`EventInstance.footprint_of()`) — and walks into view. The badge goes
off when its body box enters the visible area; a separate caret may enter first. Nothing pops
in: a test places every off-screen spawn over many bearings, camera leads, both
input schemes and a rotated window and checks its whole drawn box is out of view on its first frame.
The cyclist's first frame had shown his front view partly in view, from a placement that used the
badge icon's box; that is fixed. A covered touch-control corner is not off screen for spawning.

**The day-3 dog keeps the gold timing in the player's terms.** Its badge is 0.53s, then it spawns off
screen and closes in at its stand-off for 4.5s before it lunges; the chase — from the lunge to the
catch — is unchanged from before: 0.35s walking toward it, 0.60s standing, 2.05s walking away (2.21s
before). Seeing the dog close in for longer when she walks away is accepted ("the chase on screen
didn't change at all"). A day-3 dog with nowhere off screen to stand is logged once and owed again;
failed placements retry after one second of walking, the director's existing placement-failure
cadence, while other due events continue. The horizontal probe also preserves the walking-away
chase: 3.95s. The gold test covers both axes rather than assuming the view is square.

**A sent robber arrives chasing.** `robber_giving_chase` and `van_guard_giving_chase` spawn off screen
already pursuing after their 0.5s badge, with no closing in (`telegraph_time = 0`); the 1.5s lunge
floor stays on standing robbers only. For a sent pursuer, the separate 1.5s minimum notice before
running can shake him off starts when his body first enters the visible area, not at creation or
at an artificial age. `Tuning.TRAP_ARRIVAL_DISTANCE` is gone. The trap draws its start along the
badge's bearing first, at a front door too.

**No pursuer gives up on a walker.** `Tuning.PURSUIT_TIME` is a 30s cap rather than 3s, so walking
does not end the chase early and running still escapes. Walking away from a sent robber is caught
at about 5.0s from above and 8.9s from beside. Running from the first badge can keep a sent robber
outside the visible area for the entire pursuit; because his visible-notice clock never starts,
that chase ends at the 30s cap instead of silently shaking off an unseen arrival.

**Chosen while building, open to overturn:** a long cap rather than no end, so a pursuer held at a
wall lets go; a heavy hit as a quarter of the meter in one pass; the warning withdrawn when there is
no ground for the thing when the second is up (a fire engine summoned again while the fire is in
sight); never nearer than 180px; the cyclist from the far sidewalk where the ground just off screen
is a cross street's carriageway; a pulsing row warned down her line created at the point of its
beat that reaches her loudest (`EventManager.age_when_warned()`), without which the loose dog's
pass halved; the trap's arrival cone at 30°; the heat-investigating patrol's 4s follow left as it
was, read as outside "every pursuer" since it cannot end the day.
