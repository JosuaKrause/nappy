## M137 — The contact is whoever she hands the note to, and the trap comes to her · built 2026-09-25

*([PLAYTEST-71](../playtests/PLAYTEST-71.md): "not the first yeller she reaches but the first yeller
she interacts with" · "maybe spawn the robber in pursuing mode offscreen when she interacts with
the yeller so it runs towards her from offscreen" · "we need a version of the robber that is not
frozen when spawned".)*

**The contact.** The code already re-pointed the step onto whichever look-alike she was near;
`docs/NARRATIVE.md` now says she hands the note to him rather than reaches him first, and the
director's function is `_follow_her_between_look_alikes`, since the old name described the
overturned rule. `tests/test_resistance.gd` walks near one look-alike, away, and hands the note to
a second: the step completes on the second, he leaves, the first keeps shouting.

**The trap comes to her, for the two tasks named at the keyboard.**
`ResistanceDirector.sets_a_trap_on_her()` is true only for the man shouting (`task_event_id`
"homeless_yeller") and the van (`"delivery_van"`) — the two the player actually asked for
(PLAYTEST-71's own words for the first; PLAYTEST-144, statement 15: "After the van (day 7) a
guard chases her; after the man shouting, the robber, which is fine only if he starts off screen").
**An earlier cut of this PR had widened the trap to every perform step whose contact rides on a
row** — the burnt shell and a roadblock besides — which nobody had asked for; a semantic review
(github.com/JosuaKrause/nappy/pull/362#pullrequestreview-5326113448) caught it, and the player's
answer sent those two back to the waiting guard they had before this PR, the smallest reading.
**Not the neighbor**, whose task has never been guarded, and never a chalk mark, a bare-point task
(a door, a mast's foot, a swing, the last night's front door), the burnt shell or a roadblock,
whose robber still waits at the contact (`_maybe_set_a_trap()`).

At the handover, `ResistanceDirector._set_the_trap_on_her()` spawns `_trap_row_id()`'s row off
screen, awake from its first frame, on the guard's ground rules, preferring a bearing with a
straight walkable line to her: **`robber_giving_chase`** for the man shouting, reading the alley
robber's own numbers rather than copying them (the same body, a field of 16 over 30–200px, 130px/s,
a 30px catch, `hard_fail`, the walk-off at 100px/s; his picture is the alley robber's sprites under
a look of its own, `ROBBER_GIVING_CHASE`; his catch reads "They were waiting for you.", the alley
robber's line without the alley); **`van_guard_giving_chase`** for the van, reading the roadblock's
own guard instead (the same body, a field of 18 over 28–120px, `Tuning.HEAT_HUNTS_SPEED` (130px/s,
identical to the robber's), `hard_fail`, the robber's own 100px/s walk-off rather than
`door_guard`'s, who never leaves his post; caught, the loss line names what she was carrying: "He
caught up with the package still on her. They took her in."). Neither is ever placed, budgeted or
streamed by the scheduler: both are `SCRIPTED` with `scripted_day` 0, the marker the scheduler
already reads, and have no trigger, so `EventDef.validate()` refuses any other map-placed pursuer
built that way.

**His warning is short and his chase is long** *([PLAYTEST-140](../playtests/PLAYTEST-140.md): "12.9s
is a *long* warning to the point where nothing really happens anymore", which overturned a 12.9s
warning chosen so that walking away could not escape him)*. `telegraph_time` is
`PURSUIT_MIN_NOTICE` plus half a second, 2.0s; `duration` is twice `PURSUIT_TIME`, 6.0s — the two
rows `tests/test_events_costs.gd` carves out of the ceiling every other pursuer holds to.
**`Tuning.TRAP_ARRIVAL_DISTANCE` is 313px**, the furthest start a walker still loses from at either
row's own catch: walking straight away the gap closes at `pursue_speed` less `WALK_SPEED`
(130 − 92 = 38px/s) for both, and the van guard's catch is the tighter of the two (28px,
`MASKED_MAN_REACH`, against the robber's 30px), so the constant is stated over his row — with half
a second of the notice-plus-chase kept as margin, 28 + 38 × (2.0 + 6.0 − 0.5) = 313px; the robber's
own 30px catch would allow 315px, so 313px leaves him a hair more margin than his row alone would
need. `tests/test_resistance.gd` holds the relationships rather than the number: the screen-edge
badge is up before he is on screen, walking into him leaves the contract's reaction time between
badge and catch, and running ends the chase. On seed 4242, day 6, a real run was caught 2.1s after
the handover ("They were waiting for you."); the burst is in
`docs/evidence/m137-trap-comes-to-her-2026-09-25/`.

**Choices made where the spec was silent**: the neighbor's step, never guarded, gets no trap; a
chalk mark's robber still waits by the mark, since which side she comes from is a route decision a
chasing robber would remove (M213, the chalk mark's robber stands at the far end of its alley,
moves him within that). The same real run on the same seed ended once caught and once crying, a
race between the meter and the catch that is not this row's contract.

**Three forks were put to the player, and each has an answer:**

1. **Should a chalk mark's trap follow the same pattern as the man shouting's and the van's?** The
   player: "keep the mark's robber as is." Unchanged: a mark's guard still waits by it, woken by
   which side she approaches from.
2. **The walk-away escape on the beside-start fallback** (the wider view leaving no clear run
   above or below her, so he starts to her side instead, past which walking directly away
   outlasts his notice and chase) — explained to the player (the screen is wider than tall; a
   beside start is about 466–476px out; walking straight away from there outlasts notice plus
   chase): "that's fine" — left as built. **Measured rather than assumed**, once a review asked
   for it: over 200 seeds each (`tests/probes/m137_trap_arrival.gd`), the beside start fires for
   `robber_giving_chase` 40 times in 200 (20.0%) and for `van_guard_giving_chase` 54 times in 200
   (27.0%) — the guard's narrower 120px field leaves a tighter vertical cone (`arrival_cone()`), so
   fewer of its bearings clear a wall along the way. A rarer third case, no clear run from any
   start at all, turned up once in the robber's 200 seeds and not at all in the guard's.
3. **`duration` is `Tuning.PURSUIT_TIME × 2` (6.0s) for these two rows alone**, reopening the lever
   M91, notice per pursuer and the biker's own side of the road, recorded as tried and reverted:
   *"Raising `duration` was tried and reverted: the suite holds every pursuer's duration to
   `Tuning.PURSUIT_TIME` exactly, tighter than `validate_pursuit`'s own 2x ceiling, so that lever
   does not exist."* It exists now for these two rows only, and `tests/test_events_costs.gd` names
   them as the exception rather than hiding it. `Tuning.PURSUIT_TIME`'s own doc gives the reason
   the lever was closed: *"at `EXCITEMENT_FROM_RUNNING` a sprint is fourteen points a second, so a
   six-second chase is most of the meter and being made to run would be being made to lose."* That
   concern is the cost of sprinting, so it does not apply to a player who never runs: a runner
   sheds either row in `Tuning.PURSUIT_SHAKEN_OFF`'s own 0.35s of running, never before he is
   `Tuning.PURSUIT_MIN_NOTICE` (1.5s) old, and the doubled `duration` buys nothing against that —
   standing still, he catches her in about 2s (`_robber_giving_chase()`'s own doc), a lost day, not
   a meter cost, before the doubled seconds even begin. What the doubling buys is seconds 5–8 of
   his life, and a walker pays for that time in his field, and, from the usual start, with the
   short sprint that sheds him. From the beside start (Fork 2 above), the guard's 120px field
   never reaches a walker who left the moment he appeared (his ~476px start closes to only ~172px
   over the whole 8.0s), and the robber's 200px field reaches her only for about the last second,
   at its weak far edge. From the usual `Tuning.TRAP_ARRIVAL_DISTANCE` (313px) start above or
   below her, though — the rarer beside start is the exception, not the rule — she is inside the
   robber's field from about 3s onward: a 3.0s chase, every other pursuer's own ceiling, would
   have freed her at 5.0s, well before his 30px catch at 7.4s, so the doubled 6.0s chase is what
   makes her keep paying his field until she runs, and then pay the sprint cost to shed him before
   the catch. The trade `duration`
   pays for is the short notice PLAYTEST-140 statement 2 asked for ("nothing really happens
   anymore" stops being true), not which rows chase (PLAYTEST-144, statement 15, names that but
   not the timing). The player, asked whether the trade is fair: "let's playtest" — kept as built,
   and `docs/review/2026-09-25-hand-a-note-to-the-man.md` asks it as a played question
   rather than recording it as settled.

**The timing fork**, from a later review of this PR
(github.com/JosuaKrause/nappy/pull/362#pullrequestreview-5328807728): the merge had folded both
rows into `charging_dog`'s "not warned first" exception in `docs/EVENTS.md`, against PLAYTEST-145
statement 7 ("all offscreen events should work like that") and the events skill's own rule against
widening that exception. Asked whether to keep this PR's 2.0s notice and 6.0s chase until M226 (the
pursuing dog keeps its day-3 timing and the other warnings fit it) fits both rows to the dog's gold
timing, or retune them to that timing now: the player, "A, remove the exemption." Both rows come
off the exception list; M226's entry now names them alongside `charging_dog`; their own numbers
stand as built until that milestone lands.

**Merged with M205** (`docs/decisions/2026-09-25-M205.md`, "the note is delivered at his inner
circle; he keeps shouting after"): the man shouting now keeps shouting, and charging her, for
`ResistanceDirector.NOTE_HANDOVER_LINGER_SECONDS` (2.5s) after day 6's note is handed over, before
`leave_for_a_completed_task()` actually runs. That call and `_set_the_trap_on_her()`'s own spawn of
`robber_giving_chase` both fire from the same `_on_contact_completed()`, so the two start at the
same instant — 2.5s of the man's field against this row's 2.0s notice, so whichever way she
answers the robber, the man is still charging her for the whole of it. Neither PR's own text had
accounted for the other: standing still, the robber catches her in about 2.2s
(`_robber_giving_chase()`'s own 1.6s lunge plus 0.6s reach), before the man's own 2.5s would have
ended on its own. `docs/EVENTS.md` and `event_catalogue.gd`'s `robber_giving_chase` doc now say
so, and `docs/review/2026-09-25-hand-a-note-to-the-man.md` asks whether shouting-plus-chasing at
once — a consequence of two separate player decisions (M205's linger, M137's trap), not asked for
together by either — is more than a walker should answer at once, open to overturn rather than
settled here.
