# minty-quail — One screen-edge badge handler, testable on its own, and the stairwell badge flake found · 2026-10-25


minty-quail's observers layer, done ahead of the remaining refactor steps because a flake fails PRs at random (PR #854), answering board `stairwell-badge-flake` and inbox #852. On `main` after a docs-only PR, four checks of `tests/test_interior.gd` failed: the escape building's stairwell masked man must have his screen-edge badge up from the frame he runs until he comes into view, and it dropped early ("plain left from row 10 ... down on 31 of 156", rows 11 and 9 likewise). The same code had passed on two earlier runs. The player's answer: "there should be a central handler that provides those badges that can be tested in isolation" (inbox #852).

**Built.** `BadgeModel` (`src/ui/badge_model.gd`, a `RefCounted`) decides which dangers get a screen-edge badge, when it goes up and when it comes down. `step(delta, here, subjects, sees, walk_speed)` is handed plain `Subject` records (the row's facts the rule reads, whether it was created under a warning, whether it is charging, whether any of it is in sight, a logged name, an opaque payload for the drawing; a warning with nothing in the world yet is a `Subject` with `pending`, a place and the seconds left), her position, the clock and a `sees(point, margin_px)` Callable. It owns the per-instance state (last position, smoothed approach, hold, seen) and answers `coming` and `announcing()`. The thresholds are the same numbers (`CLOSING_SPEED` 20, `LEAD_TIME` 5, `HOLD` 0.8, `SMOOTHING` 6, `SCREEN_MARGIN` 130, `MOST_AT_ONCE` 3). `DangerEdge` keeps the drawing and the per-frame hand-off: `_measure()` builds the `Subject`s from the event source's `instances()` and `pending_warnings()` and the view, and calls the model. The call site (`main._add_danger_edge()`) is unchanged. The seam for events-composed's planned `WarningBoard`: it hands the model `Subject`s and a view Callable and reads the badges back; the model knows no `EventInstance`, `PendingWarning`, `VisibleView` or node.

**What varied.** The stairwell checks depend on `Engine.get_physics_interpolation_fraction()` at the moment a case starts. `project.godot` has physics interpolation on at 30 ticks per second, so the camera's view centre, which the badge asks, is shifted by a wall-clock fraction of a camera step (tens of world px) in a suite that steps the camera by hand. When the masked man sets off from inside the 130 screen px (65 world px) out-of-sight band past the view's edge, the old rule never raised his badge (`not sees(at, margin)` with `seen` already true) and it stayed down until he left the band.
Measured, on one machine (macOS, Godot 4.7.2), before the change: plain `tools/test.sh --serial interior` passed 5 of 5 and a local `--shard 5/8` passed (125380 checks, 0 failures). Sleeping inside the loop, camera smoothing speed 0.1x to 10x, and the idle-frame delta reaching the suite (0.005 to about 0.29 s) changed nothing. With a temporary, uncommitted loop starting each of the 12 stairwell cases after a random 0 to 90 ms pause and a process frame, 4 of 7 runs had failures; in the 3 failing runs where the fraction was logged, every failing case started at 0.906, 0.928 or 0.928, while the roughly 40 logged passing cases covered 0.19 to 0.95. The failures were the row 10 and 11 walk-pace cases and a row 11 run-pace case, the same shape as CI's. With the test camera's `physics_interpolation_mode` off, the same loop over 6 runs of 12 cases had 0 drops.
Shown: the failure follows the interpolation fraction in this loop, and removing the camera's interpolation removes it. Suggestive, not proven: that the failing starts cluster at a high fraction (three logged data points), and that CI's failure was this fraction (CI's was never observed).

**Verified at test parity** (inbox #813 in curly-moose): at `b4cd1abd`, `check.sh`, `lint.sh`, `ci_telemetry_kinds.py` and `cost-table.sh --check` pass, and the whole suite shard by shard (`TEST_SHARD_TIMEOUT_S=2400`, one at a time) had 0 failures and no `SCRIPT ERROR` line: shards 1 to 8 ran 86599, 58065, 492324, 16499, 117505, 134890, 268260 and 122522 checks. Tests changed only as far as the code moved: new `tests/test_badge_model.gd` (238 checks, no scene) covers approach from off screen, walking away or too slow, the hold, a seen thing in the band, a charger setting off inside the band, the masked man's run from five starts past the view edge (5, 25, 45, 64 and 90 px), warned arrival, a pending warning, the cap of three and its order, pruning and which rows are worth an arrow; with the charger rule taken out 5 of them fail. In `tests/test_interior.gd` the stairwell badge checks run with the test camera not interpolated, and a guard runs the four walk cases with interpolation on from 44 seeded starts across the tick and checks the fractions differed; with the charger rule taken out it fails 2 of the 44 while the non-interpolated checks pass.

**Choices made where the design was silent, open to overturn:** the name `BadgeModel` and one `Subject` record for live things and pending warnings; the constants, `approach_speed()`, `announces()`, `_is_worth_an_arrow*()`, `_coming` and `_watch` stay on `DangerEdge` as forwarders because tests and `tests/probes/m207_warning_lead.gd` still name them; the guard's pauses are a fixed list rather than random per run; and the fix is both the handler rule and the deterministic test, the orchestrating session's pick, not the brief's.

**For the remeasuring step:** one deliberate behaviour change. A charger that has just begun charging (`EventInstance.has_charged()`, its first moving frame) resets its `seen` flag, so its badge is raised when it is outside the view proper (margin 0) rather than outside the margin band, which comes back once it has been in sight again; the hysteresis stays for everything else. It is in the shared rule for every charger, not keyed on the masked man. A charger that starts within 65 world px past the view's edge now has its badge from the frame it runs; before, it ran without one until it left the band. Nothing else is meant to change.
