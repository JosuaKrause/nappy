# M219 — On a pedestrian street a blocked walker turns round · 2026-10-11


[PLAYTEST-143](../playtests/PLAYTEST-143.md), statement 5: "on the pedestrian street people don't
turn around when their path is blocked so they accumulate on obstacles"; re-reported on v0.27.1,
seed 67, day 9 ([velvet-marten](../playtests/2026-10-11-velvet-marten.md), #696): "I noticed people
still pile up in the pedestrian zone instaed of turning around at barriers."

**The cause.** On a precinct `CrowdAgent._footway_is_shut()` never answered true, since a precinct
is stepped round across all six lanes rather than shut lane by lane, so `_turn_round()` was never
reached there. When stepping round gave up, the walker slowed to a stop at the obstacle and the
ones behind it queued.

**Built: a precinct is shut to a walker by reachability.** `_footway_is_shut()`
(`src/crowd/crowd_agent.gd`) hands a walker on a precinct to `_precinct_way_is_shut()`, which runs
only when the probed tile is itself blocked. It walks the rows ahead, up to `BODY_SCAN_TILES`, from
the walker's own tile: the lanes it holds that are open in the next row carry straight on and then
spread sideways through open lanes; a row with no blocked lane past the first body is a way out; if
no lane carries, the way is shut and `_cannot_go_on()` reports a wall, so the existing `_divert()` →
`_turn_round()` path turns the walker round, as on an ordinary street. The walk is worked out once per
look-ahead scan (`_precinct_way_is_shut_cached()`). A blockage with a way through is still stepped
round across all six lanes, except a zig-zag that needs a shift of three lanes or more inside one
clear row, which holds walkers in front of it exactly as before this change: that is tawny-bison.
An ordinary footway is unchanged.
`docs/MECHANICS.md` says so beside the ordinary footway's rule.

**Rejected: "all six lanes taken in one row".** It was the first revision, and review found that
partial bodies in neighbouring rows leave no way through without filling any one row: seed 67's
day 3, east-west precinct, café tables in lanes 4–5, construction in lanes 1–3 and a soft seal in
lanes 0, 1, 4 and 5 on three consecutive rows. There it made the pile-up worse than before.

**Measured** with `tests/probes/m219_precinct_pile.gd`, 30 s, walkers within 1.5 tiles of the
blockage, most at once / mean per frame:

| | before | one-row rule | reachability |
|---|---|---|---|
| seed 67 day 3, (10,156) | 8 / 6.67 | 18 / 11.18 | 4 / 0.98 |
| seed 24757 day 5, (122,156) | 8 / 5.62 | 12 / 6.53 | 2 / 0.47 |

What remains is walkers inside the stride they take to turn. The second review swept 207 precinct
blockages (seed 67's days 1 to 14 and eight more seeds on days 3, 6, 9 and 12): none holds a walker
standing three seconds or more, and its cost is about 1–2% of the crowd's step. On seed 67's day 9, the day the player
reported, the review found two things standing in a precinct at dawn: café tables in two lanes,
stepped round before and after, and a collapsed frontage across all six lanes of the north–south
precinct, where 8 walkers stood before and none stand now; events placed later in the day were not
reproduced.

*Open to overturn, chosen by the implementer:* lanes carry straight on, never diagonally; the
bodies counted are `CityMap.obstructed_tiles`, closed tiles and soft seals, and bollard posts, which
have no crowd effect, stay so.

**Verified** by two tests in `tests/test_crowd_bodies.gd`: a precinct walled across its width, and
one blocked by the staggered shape above (lanes 0, 1, 4, 5, then 1–3, then 4–5), each walking six
walkers, one per lane, at it for 20 seconds. The staggered one fails on the one-row rule (no walker
turns, 6720 of 7200 walker-frames spent in front of the bodies) and passes now; the full-width one
fails on the code before either. The review's finding that seals on a precinct leave its middle two
lanes open is its own entry, tiny-alpaca.
