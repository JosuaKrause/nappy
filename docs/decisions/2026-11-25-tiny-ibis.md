# tiny-ibis — A waiting charger warns only where she sees it, the covered corners left out · 2026-11-25

In the joystick scheme the steering ring and the Run disc cover the view's two bottom corners, and what she can see
already leaves them out (`VisibleView`; feathery-lynx #581, which asks every place where visibility matters to follow
it, and the tap scheme to keep the whole view). A charger already in the world was not held to it: the waiting dog, the
alley robber and the hunting roadblock's guard noticed her once the whole of their warning picture was inside the
camera's whole view, corners included, and a vehicle letting men out stopped once it and every place its men may get
out were inside that same rect (bouncy-owl #1774's fix for the vehicle, azure-hawk). So a hand could go up under the
Run disc, with no badge and no warning she could see. The defect is finding 4 of the Opus review of PR #1785.

**"In her view" for an on-screen charger is now "all of it in sight".** `VisibleView.sees_all(rect)` answers it:
inside the view and, while the joystick scheme is in force, meeting neither covered corner.
`EventInstance._would_stand_in_her_view()`, the one check the waiting charger's notice (`EventPursuit`) and the
vehicle's stop (`EventInvestigation`) already asked, asks it of the warning picture, and of the vehicle's drawn box and
each man's raised-hand picture at every place he may get out. A charger under a covered corner keeps waiting until she
has him in sight, inside his radius, or walks on. The check stays the one place the decision is made; nothing repairs a
warning after it has gone up.

**What she can see is handed to the event, not re-derived.** `WhereSheIs`, the tick's word every live event hears,
carried the camera's rect (`Rect2`); it now carries the day's one `VisibleView`, which `EventManager` looks through
the camera at the top of the tick and tells with the rest of the word. The covered corners are laid onto the view once
a frame in the object that owns them, and no event works them out again from a rect and a cover. `WhereSheIs.view()`
answers null where nothing has looked through a camera (a rig that steps an instance directly), which, as before, leaves
the notice to the radius alone. Every test and rig that told `Rect2()` tells null; the views a test told are
`VisibleView`s in the tap scheme, so none of their numbers moved.

**The pop-in rule is untouched.** A thing still never appears or vanishes anywhere in the camera's whole view, corners
included (`SpawnSiting.on_the_view()`, olive-hedgehog #598), so a charger under a corner is neither made to arrive
already charging there nor taken away.

**Rejected:** a second test beside the notice, asking `EventManager.sees()` of the warning picture's corners after the
whole-rect test. It would be two answers to one question, and the point test needs the picture's four corners to stand
for the box (true only because a covered corner touches the view's own corner); `sees_all()` asks the box itself.
Handing each event the `ScreenCover` with the rect was rejected for the same reason: every event would lay the corners
onto the view again.

**Held by the tests:** a robber whose warning picture stands 10px into the bottom-left corner of a view led by her walk up and
to the right (she inside his radius) waits in the joystick scheme and warns in the tap one; the
fully heated unmarked van with its lower door's men 10px into that corner (she inside its notice) does not stop in
the joystick scheme and owes its two men in the tap one. Both cases fail with the corners ignored.

**Not covered, open:** day 13's task guard set on her when the roadblock task is done
(`ResistanceDirector._set_the_task_guard_on_her()`) warns her in place whenever any of him is drawn on the camera's
whole view, and is sent under a badge only when none of him is. Standing under a covered corner, or half past the
view's edge, he raises his hand where she may not see it. Neither of the two answers fits him there (a badge would
need him taken away in the camera's view, which is pop-in), so it is left for a decision: hold the order until he is in
sight or off the view, or accept it.
