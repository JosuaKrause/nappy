## M205 — The halo revert, and the meter's still-open reproduction · 2026-09-27

*(PLAYTEST-140, statement 4: "the yeller has no effect on the meter and its halo doesn't even turn
on." PLAYTEST-144, statement 21: the man shouting had no effect on the meter on an ordinary day,
not only on the day of the note. Statements 23, 24 and 27, with
`docs/evidence/playtest-144-phone-v0.18.0-2026-09-26/halo-on-a-passer-by.png`,
`yeller-no-halo-no-charge.png` and `desktop-chrome-halo-on-a-car-yeller-none.png`: the halo draws
on a passer-by on the phone and on a car in desktop Chrome, on the released page, and draws on
neither the man shouting on the phone nor beside her doorstep on the desktop — desktop Chrome on
the released page is the Web export, the same build the phone runs, not a native build.)*

**Chased and reverted: a per-`EntityHalo`-material rewrite, on no real bug.** An earlier session
on this branch rewrote `EntityHalo`'s shared `ShaderMaterial` into one material per rim, on the
strength of a debug Web export — built and served locally, never the released page — that computed
a real target alpha for a live `homeless_yeller` while no rim drew, and concluded the halo had been
broken on every Web export since M89 ("the halo's `instance uniform` on one shared material"). That
conclusion is wrong: v0.10.0, tested the same way, shows the identical non-draw on this Mac's own
local debug export, while the *released* page — the one the screenshots above are actually taken
on — draws a car's halo correctly with the *original*, unmodified shared-material code. So the
local debug export's non-draw was an artefact of that export, not a fact about the shipped game.
The per-rim-material rewrite is reverted: `src/ui/entity_halo.gd`,
`assets/shaders/excitement_halo.gdshader` and `tests/test_halo.gd` are back to `origin/main`'s own
text, and `docs/evidence/m205-halo-web-2026-09-26/` (the now-unsupported before/after stills from
the local export) is deleted. M89 is not overturned: `instance uniform` on one shared material is
still what serves every rim in the game.

**Not reproduced anywhere: the man shouting himself with no halo and no charge.** Three faithful
simulations — a bare `EventInstance` queried directly, a real `City` + `EventManager` streamed the
way `main.gd` does, on five seeds, and a full boot of `scenes/main.tscn` walking toward a live
`homeless_yeller` — all charge him correctly and draw his halo once he clears
`ExcitementHalo.CONTRIBUTION_FLOOR` (1.0/s). **All three are native or headless.** Every sighting of
the symptom — both playtests above, and both stills — is on the Web export; none of the
reproductions here has actually been tried on one.

**Two candidates were raised and do not survive the player's own stills.** `ExcitementHalo.
select_sources()`'s `MAX_SOURCES` (8) cap, strongest contribution first, could leave a source's rim
dark on a busy crosswalk while the meter — which sums every live source, uncapped — still receives
its contribution; but both stills show *fewer than eight rims lit in total* (one, on the desktop
still's car; none, on the phone still), so a cap that only bites past eight competing sources cannot
be what is silencing his one. And the report or the screenshot being made during his 2.6s
`telegraph_time` (`Tuning.TELEGRAPH_INTENSITY_FRACTION`, 15% of his ordinary intensity, sometimes
under the halo's own floor) can explain at most one single frame, not two separately reported
sightings across two playtests. Neither candidate is the cause; both are set aside rather than
carried forward as open guesses.

**Next step, concrete rather than "if this recurs" (it already has): a local *release* Web
export.** `tools/serve-web.sh` (or the export CI publishes, not a debug export built and served
locally) walked beside a live `homeless_yeller`, reading his `contribution_at()`,
`ExcitementHalo.select_sources()` and the meter's own rate in the browser while it happens, since
that is the one difference between every sighting and every reproduction in this tree — the build,
not the emission or the halo code, which the three simulations above already charge and light
correctly.
