## M205 — The note is delivered at his inner circle; he keeps shouting after · 2026-09-27

*(PLAYTEST-144, statement 22: "Handing over a task's note costs excitement. A task is an extra
challenge about managing excitement and is deliberately dangerous; the ordinary route is the
almost free path." A fork proposed making her stand inside his field for a while before the note
completes, and the player rejected it outright: "the player should stand for 2.5s? no way. the
moment the player touches the inner circle it counts as delivered." Asked next how an instant
handover should still cost something — a fixed charge, him shouting a while longer, or dropping the
ask — the player answered: "he keeps shouting for a bit.")*

**The bug.** Day 6's note handover (`ResistanceSteps.by_index(2)`, riding a live `homeless_yeller`)
completed the instant `ContactPoint.REACH` (36px) was reached and he left at once — a fixed plumbing
constant every task's contact shares, which happens to sit inside his 45px `inner_radius` (his own
full-strength field), and a departure that stopped him charging her the moment the note changed
hands. So the whole handover only ever charged the last few pixels of the approach: ~4.7 points
awake (`tests/probes/m205_note_handover.gd`, averaged over eight points in his own pulse), against
~11.1 for an ordinary full pass doing nothing in particular (`M174Pass.pass_net_averaged()` at 0px,
the same simulation `docs/COSTS.md`'s "the pass — awake" row is generated from).

**What is built, in two parts.** First, `ResistanceSteps.Step.completes_at_inner_radius` (true only
for this one step) makes `ContactPoint._physics_process()` complete the instant she is within the
rider's own `inner_radius` instead of `REACH` — still instant, not a dwell. A first version of this
half tied the handover to a continuous 2.5s stand inside `inner_radius`
(`Tuning.NOTE_HANDOVER_DWELL_SECONDS`); the player rejected it, quoted above, before it reached
`main`, so the dwell and its constant never merged. Second,
`ResistanceDirector.NOTE_HANDOVER_LINGER_SECONDS` (2.5s) delays `EventInstance.
leave_for_a_completed_task()`: the man she reached stays and keeps shouting, still charging her
exactly as any live `homeless_yeller` does, for that long before he actually leaves
(`ResistanceDirector._tick_lingering_rider()`). Walking away from him afterward is what makes the
task cost, not the moment the note itself changes hands.

**Measured.** Completing at `inner_radius` alone, with no linger, costs *less* than completing at
`REACH` did: `inner_radius` (45px) sits *outside* `REACH` (36px), so stopping there ends the
approach a few pixels *earlier* — `tests/probes/m205_note_handover.gd` measures ~4.1 points awake at
`inner_radius` against ~4.7 at `REACH`, both well under the ~11.1 an ordinary pass costs. What that
half buys on its own is tying the handover to a fact about the row itself (his own full-strength
field) rather than a generic constant that happened to sit inside it — it does not by itself close
the gap to an ordinary pass. The linger is what does: walking away from him for
`NOTE_HANDOVER_LINGER_SECONDS` at `Tuning.WALK_SPEED` clears his `outer_radius` (210px, past which
he contributes nothing) with a margin, so the total plateaus rather than depending on an exact
frame — measured at that plateau, ~11.1 points awake and ~-3.2 asleep, against the ~11.1/~-3.2 an
ordinary pass costs. `tests/test_resistance.gd`,
`_test_the_notes_handover_completes_at_his_inner_radius_not_reach`, pins the instant completion at
`inner_radius` and the fact that an ordinary step's plain `REACH` check does not fire at the same
distance; `_test_completing_the_yeller_step_sends_only_its_rider_away` and
`_test_a_lost_day_still_offers_the_mark_and_then_the_yeller_on_retry` pin that he keeps shouting
right after the handover and only leaves once the linger is actually up.

**Applies to every task whose handover rides a man shouting.** Only day 6's note does today
(`ResistanceSteps._build()`); both fields live on `Step`/`ResistanceDirector` rather than on index 2
specifically, so a later task built the same way (`_perform()`, riding `homeless_yeller`) inherits
the same fix by sharing the same `task_event_id` check rather than needing a second one.
